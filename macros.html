How it's calculated

Pick a calorie target, then a split. Balanced is 30% protein, 40% carbohydrate and 30% fat. High protein is 40 / 30 / 30. Low carb is 30 / 20 / 50. Custom is whatever three percentages you enter, as long as they add up to 100.

Grams are calories divided by 4 for protein, by 4 for carbohydrate, and by 9 for fat. At 2,500 kcal on the balanced split, protein is 750 kcal and 187.5 g, carbohydrate is 1,000 kcal and 250 g, and fat is 750 kcal and 83.3 g.

If you set protein from body weight, grams of protein are kilograms times the g-per-kg figure. Those grams become protein calories (× 4). Carbohydrate and fat share whatever calories remain, using their ratio from the preset. On the balanced preset that ratio is 40:30.

FAQ

What is the balanced split?

30% of calories from protein, 40% from carbohydrate and 30% from fat. It is a common middle setting, not a requirement.

Why 4 and 9?

Those are the Atwater figures used on labels: about 4 kcal per gram of protein, 4 per gram of carbohydrate, and 9 per gram of fat.

What does grams per kilogram do?

It fixes protein from body weight, often somewhere in 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg for people who train. The rest of the calories are split between carbohydrate and fat.

Do the grams have to match every day?

No. They are a target for planning. A single meal, or a single day, does not have to land on the gram.
