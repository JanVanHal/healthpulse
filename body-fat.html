How it's calculated

The US Navy circumference equations estimate body density, then turn density into a fat percentage with the Siri equation: 495 ÷ density − 450. This page uses the centimetre form published by Hodgdon and Beckett.

For men, density is 1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height). For a man at 187 cm, with a 40 cm neck and a 95 cm waist, that comes out at about 20.7%.

For women, density is 1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height). The waist must be larger than the neck for men. For women, the waist plus the hip must be larger than the neck.

The category uses the bands in the table: essential, athletes, fitness, average and obese. A tape and an equation are not a DEXA scan.

FAQ

What is the Navy method?

A screening formula from height and tape measurements. The military used it to estimate body fat without a lab.

Where do I measure?

Neck just below the larynx, waist at the navel, and, for women, the hip at the widest point. Measure against bare skin, standing relaxed, tape level.

Why might a scale disagree?

Scales, calipers and scans each have their own error. A 20% from one method and 23% from another can both be honest estimates.

What do the categories mean?

They are descriptive bands, not a diagnosis. Essential is the low end, athletes and fitness sit below average, and obese is the high band in that table.
