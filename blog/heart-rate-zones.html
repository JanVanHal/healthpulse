Heart-rate zones for beginners

TrainingAugust 20, 2026·3 min read

By HealthPulse team

A zone is a percentage of an estimated maximum. The maximum is itself an estimate. Treat both as a map, not a fence.

Two estimates of max

The older rule is 220 minus age. At 51 that is 169 bpm. Tanaka’s formula, 208 − 0.7 × age, gives 172 bpm at the same age (208 − 35.7 rounds to 172). The heart-rate calculator shows both. Tanaka tends to be a little kinder to older adults and a little lower for younger ones. Neither formula has met you. A max measured in a proper test beats both, if you have one and a clinician is happy for you to do the test.

Five bands

Zones on this site are slices of the Tanaka max. Zone 1 is 50–60%, easy effort for warm-up and cool-down. Zone 2 is 60–70%, where you can still talk in sentences: the bulk of easy aerobic work. Zone 3 is 70–80%, comfortably hard, the tempo range. Zone 4 is 80–90%, hard, near the point where the effort starts to climb on its own. Zone 5 is 90–100%, very hard, and only for short efforts.

At a max of 172, zone 2 is roughly 103 to 120 bpm. The edges are rounded, so neighbouring zones can share a beat. That does not matter. The point of the band is the effort, not the last digit.

Karvonen, if you know your resting pulse

A low resting heart rate means more room between rest and max. Karvonen uses that room, called the reserve. Reserve is max minus resting. A zone is then resting plus the reserve times the same percentage. Someone resting at 50 with a max of 172 has a reserve of 122. Seventy percent is 50 + 85, about 135 bpm, which is higher than 70% of 172. The page fills a Karvonen column only when you enter a resting rate.

You can count resting pulse in the morning, before coffee, for 30 or 60 seconds. One morning is a noisy reading. A week of mornings is better. Leave the box blank if you do not know it. The percentage-of-max zones still work.

What zones are not

They are not a medical limit, and they are not a reason to ignore dizziness, chest pain or a heart condition. Talk to a doctor before a big change in training. Use the zones to keep easy days easy. Most people do their hard running too hard and their easy running too hard as well. A ceiling on the easy days is the useful part.
