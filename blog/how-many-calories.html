How many calories do you really need?

EnergySeptember 12, 2026·3 min read

By HealthPulse team

A daily calorie number feels exact. It is an equation, then a guess about how active the day is.

Resting burn, then the rest of the day

Most of the calories in a quiet day are the ones you burn while doing very little: breathing, circulating blood, keeping warm. That resting figure is the basal metabolic rate, or BMR. The rest is movement, from fidgeting up to training. A calculator splits the problem in two. First the BMR, then a multiplier for the kind of week you have.

The calorie calculator uses Mifflin-St Jeor for the BMR. For men it is 10 × kilograms + 6.25 × centimetres − 5 × age + 5. For women the last term is − 161 instead of + 5. A 51-year-old man at 90 kg and 187 cm lands on 1,818.75 kcal, which rounds to 1,819.

The activity factor is blunt

The page then multiplies by a factor: 1.2 for a sedentary day, 1.375 for light exercise a few days a week, 1.55 for moderate, 1.725 for hard exercise most days, and 1.9 for a physical job or twice-daily training. Light activity on that example is 1,818.75 × 1.375, which is 2,501 kcal after rounding. That is the “maintain” figure.

The factor is a category, not a diary. Two people who both pick “light” do not have the same day. If you already counted a workout in the factor, do not add the same workout again on top.

Loss and gain targets

A common planning step is to subtract 250 kcal for a mild loss, or 500 for a larger one, and to add the same amounts for gain. On paper, 500 kcal a day is roughly half a kilogram a week, because a kilogram of body fat is often treated as about 7,700 kcal. Bodies do not cash that cheque exactly. Water, muscle and a metabolism that adapts all get in the way. The targets are still useful as a starting gap between intake and the estimate.

A target under about 1,200 kcal is low enough that it should be checked with a doctor before anyone tries to eat it. The equation will still print the number. Printing is not permission.

From calories to grams

Once you have a target, the macro calculator can split it. Protein and carbohydrate are about 4 kcal per gram, fat about 9. A 30 / 40 / 30 split of 2,500 kcal is 187.5 g of protein, 250 g of carbohydrate and 83.3 g of fat. Those grams are a plan for the week, not a score you have to hit at every meal.
