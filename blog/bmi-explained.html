BMI explained: what it tells you and what it doesn't

BodyOctober 1, 2026·3 min read

By HealthPulse team

BMI is a ratio, not a verdict. It is useful because it is simple, and it is limited for the same reason.

The arithmetic

Body mass index is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. Take someone who is 187 cm and 90 kg. Height in metres is 1.87. Squared, that is 3.4969. 90 divided by 3.4969 is 25.7. The BMI calculator does that division, and it converts pounds and feet-and-inches first so the result does not depend on the unit you think in.

The number has no unit that matters in daily use. A taller heavier person and a shorter lighter person can share it. That is the point of dividing by height squared: it puts different bodies on one scale.

The bands

The World Health Organization adult bands are under 18.5 (underweight), 18.5 to 24.9 (normal), 25 to 29.9 (overweight) and 30 and above (obese). 25.7 sits in the overweight band. A WHO expert consultation also published action points of 23 and 27.5 for some Asian populations, because the health risk associated with BMI can start earlier in those groups. 25.7 is above 23 and below 27.5, so it is in that overweight band as well.

Bands are drawn on populations. They are how researchers sort thousands of people. They are not a diagnosis of any one person, and they were not designed to tell athletes from anyone else.

What the ratio cannot see

BMI does not know how much of the weight is muscle, bone or fat. It does not know whether fat sits under the skin or around the organs. It does not know fitness, blood pressure, sleep or what you eat. Two people at 25.7 can have very different health, and a person at 23 can be unwell while a person at 27 is not.

That is why a single BMI is a screen, not a check-up. If the number surprises you, it is a reason to look at the rest of the picture, including a conversation with a doctor, not a reason to treat the band name as a label.

A practical way to use it

Use it to notice a change. If your height is stable, a move from 24 to 28 is a real change in weight, whatever the band is called. Use the Asian cut-offs as extra context if they apply to you, and ignore them if they do not. And do not compare your BMI with someone else’s as if the lower number won.
